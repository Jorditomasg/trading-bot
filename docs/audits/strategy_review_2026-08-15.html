Incident Critical Fixed & deployed

A dead bot that looked perfectly healthy

On 13 Aug 02:58 UTC a connection reset during a Binance SSL handshake escaped the position manager, propagated out of the scheduler, and killed the main loop. The container stayed Up with a restart count of zero — because the websocket manager runs non-daemon threads, the process never exited, so Docker's restart policy never fired.

Nothing ran for 2 days 8 hours. No entries, and — the part that matters — no position manager, which is the only thing watching stops on open positions.

What it cost

The open SOLUSDT trade (entry 76.42, stop 75.089) had its stop breached on 14 Aug at 14:00 — session low 74.69 — and no exit was executed. Entries stopping is harmless. Exits stopping is not.

Three things hid it: the process didn't exit, the logs kept scrolling with warnings from surviving daemon threads, and the crash handler logged and returned by design. Only the absence of INFO lines gave it away.

Cheapest liveness probe

“Container Up” proves nothing. The equity table gets a row per hourly cycle, so SELECT MAX(timestamp) FROM equity is the honest health check.

Fixed with a per-job guard wrapper plus a contained scheduler tick and try/finally cleanup, so the process now actually exits and lets Docker restart it. Five regression tests pin the behaviour — including one that deliberately pins the unguarded starvation case, so the reason for per-job wrapping can't be refactored away.

Measurement Documented

The documented risk table described a strategy that wasn't running

A research script built its backtest config by hand, which silently inherits the permissive research defaults. That config differed from production in ten fields — all four entry-quality filters off instead of on, the strict bias gate off, Kelly sizing on instead of off, and a take-profit of 4.5 instead of the seeded 5.0. Its output was written into the project's own baseline documentation.

The stop is well chosen. Read down the 5.0 column: 0.62 → 1.16 → 1.23 → 1.18 → 1.08. Cleanly centred on 1.5 with no cliff either side, and the same shape holds in every column. That is what a parameter that isn't curve-fit looks like.

The take-profit is not. Along the 1.5 row, Calmar climbs all the way to the edge of the grid and never turns over. The optimum lies outside the tested range — and the built-in walk-forward optimizer caps its take-profit grid at exactly 5.0, so it cannot discover the better region even in principle.

Deliberately not acting on this

A monotonic climb into a boundary is exactly what a plain grid search over-reads, and higher targets also mean fewer trades and less fee drag — two explanations, one number. The right next step is widening the grid and running the walk-forward audit, not editing a config.

Hypothesis Rejected

The obvious fix for the drawdown makes it worse

The strategy ships two entry gates that are switched off and that look like the natural answer to a bear market: block longs below the 200 EMA, and require a minimum trend strength. Judging them on the current bear alone would be regime-fitting, so they were tested across five regime windows.

The live setting wins or ties in every window. The striking result is the 2022 column: the 200-EMA gate makes the bear market itself worse — Calmar −0.34 to −0.52, win rate 23.5% down to 18.8%. It barely removes any trades (34 → 32) but it delays re-entry on the recovery legs, which is precisely where a long-only trend follower earns the money that pays for the bear.

Action: none. Both gates stay off. This is the fix people reach for during a drawdown, and it has now been tested and ruled out.

Context

Is the live losing streak the edge failing?

The live record reads badly: 12 closed trades, one win, profit factor 0.22, −231 USDT on a 10,000 baseline. Three independent checks say environment, not breakage.

  • Regime scoring puts the market at RISK_OFF, 37.4/100. Bitcoin sits in a bear stack — price below the 50-day below the 200-day, with the 200-day falling — 49.5% off its one-year high, with only 35% of the top 20 positive over 30 days.
  • The backtest reproduces it. 2026 in the table above is −10.2% with a 19.5% win rate, on data the strategy never saw.
  • The sample is too small to conclude anything. Twelve trades is below the 30-trade minimum. The probability of one win or fewer in twelve, at the true 34% rate, is 4.9% — low, but not damning once the regime is accounted for.
The live sample is contaminated anyway

Four of those twelve trades were opened by code that traded the still-forming candle, and the open SOL trade spans the multi-day outage. A clean live scorecard starts from today.

Open items

What still needs doing

  • Nothing is committed. Both production fixes run from a local image CI has never built. An image prune or a compose revert silently undoes both. Highest priority.
  • Widen the take-profit grid past 5.0 and re-run the walk-forward audit before touching the parameter.
  • No liveness alerting. The outage went unnoticed for two days and eight hours. A daily heartbeat exists, but nothing alerts on its absence — and silence is indistinguishable from a healthy quiet bot.
  • The one-decision-per-bar guard is in-memory. A restart clears it, and the host reboots weekly. Low impact, not zero.
Nine tunable parameters is the one medium red flag in the scoring — curve-fitting surface worth remembering before adding a tenth.
